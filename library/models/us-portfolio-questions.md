<h2 id="supplier">Follow the question, then check what you counted</h2>
<p>Start with the purple supplier records, follow the links to green investments, then look at the orange organisations responsible for them. The original reports 16 investments/programmes across nine departments for IBM. That is a historical result, not a newly verified total. The source itself explains that separate supplier codes represent the same supplier.</p>
<p>The useful move is from <strong>“show me the portfolio”</strong> to <strong>“show me the relationships relevant to this decision”</strong>. It lets a manager inspect common supplier exposure without drawing every project at once. A recorded link establishes an association in this model; it does not establish criticality, capacity or the effect of a supplier failure.</p>
<p>The next question follows investment-to-metric links: what measures are being used on this work? The original query groups measurement categories. A count of matched rows can repeat an investment or metric when several paths reach it. Counting distinct programmes would be a different question requiring a clear identifier and counting rule.</p>

<h2 id="model">One model, several useful views</h2>
<p>The schema is the key to the pictures. Its node types distinguish agencies, bureaus, investments, projects, activities, contracts, suppliers, metrics, services and business functions. The main ownership/delivery chain reads <strong>agency → bureau → investment → project → activity</strong>. Other links connect delivery work to suppliers, measures and its organisational purpose.</p>
<figure class="pw-framework-figure"><a href="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lawrencerowland/Data-models-for-portfolios/fa79aed9e5e7a83391de3b65c7067d5c4a13bb23/Example_0_a_thousand_US_projects/images1/schema.png"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lawrencerowland/Data-models-for-portfolios/fa79aed9e5e7a83391de3b65c7067d5c4a13bb23/Example_0_a_thousand_US_projects/images1/schema.png" width="774" height="458" loading="lazy" alt="Original schema connecting Agency, Bureau, Investment, Project and Activity with Supplier, Contract, Metric, Service and business classification nodes."></a><figcaption>The original schema names the kinds of records and relationships. It is a model of this dataset, not a universal project ontology. Select the picture for the full-size original.</figcaption></figure>
<p><code>Investment</code> is the dataset’s type name. It can include programmes or operational expenditure, so substituting “programme” everywhere would change the meaning. Business functions and services are also distinct classifications, not two labels for the same level.</p>
<h3>Zoom into the Food and Drug Administration</h3>
<p>The FDA view takes one bureau and the records connected to its investments. Read outward into projects and activities, and sideways into suppliers, contracts, metrics and classifications. The same records can support a bureau view, a supplier view or a question about late projects.</p>

<h3 id="counting-records">Why 437 investments and 721 investments can both appear</h3>
<p>The full export contains <strong>7,249 Investment records, of which 437 have names</strong>, and <strong>1,910 Project records, of which 1,547 have names</strong>. The walkthrough’s headline figures match those named subsets. Its queries often explicitly require an investment name.</p>
<p>The Health export contains <strong>721 Investment records, only 22 named</strong>, and <strong>202 Project records, 108 named</strong>. The hierarchy draft uses the broader counts. Static inspection also finds unique stored investment identifiers within each export; these particular totals are not evidence of duplicate identifiers.</p>
<p>This is a concrete reason to ask “which records are included?” before comparing totals. We inspected the <a href="https://github.com/lawrencerowland/Data-models-for-portfolios/blob/master/Example_0_a_thousand_US_projects/cypher_code/Complete_graph.cypher">complete</a> and <a href="https://github.com/lawrencerowland/Data-models-for-portfolios/blob/master/Example_0_a_thousand_US_projects/cypher_code/Health_sub_graph.cypher">Health</a> export text; we did not rebuild the database or independently validate the real-world identities.</p>

<h2 id="limits">What the original results do—and do not—show</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Late projects and missing detail.</strong> The HR example reaches activities through delayed projects, then discovers missing activity records. A sparse picture can reveal missing data; it does not establish that the unshown work had no activities. Its text also switches between 6/8 programmes and 20/17 delayed projects.</li>
<li><strong>Delay is not a causal explanation.</strong> Summed project-delay days are not the elapsed delay of the portfolio. A small average start delay does not establish why activities finished late. The source’s suggestion that reliability explains delay is untested.</li>
<li><strong>Ratings and measures have specific meanings.</strong> The low-score query uses <code>Evaluation_by_CIO</code>, not client satisfaction. The query for projects more than 100 days late counts metric-description occurrences in matched rows, not distinct programmes. Several projects can repeat the same metric.</li>
<li><strong>Money and totals need reconciliation.</strong> The £82m caption conflicts with <code>Enhancement_spend_$m</code>; no conversion is documented. Exported nodes, unique business identifiers and filtered query rows are different units. The walkthrough’s totals should not be silently reconciled with a different saved export.</li>
</ul>
<p>These limits preserve a useful lesson: a graph makes relationships inspectable, but the meaning of a result still depends on identifiers, missing data, units and the question used to select it.</p>

<h2 id="governance">Start with the governance need, then choose the tools</h2>
<p>A companion October 2020 note asks how an organisation’s ambitions, accountabilities and existing information should shape implementation. It works from <strong>governance needs → management information → data → integration → tools and processes</strong>. The graph can be a place to develop the model with people, even when the eventual operating system uses a relational database.</p>
