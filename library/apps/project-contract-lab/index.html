1Scope statement · what is being provided?

Scenario: the Contractor will install a modern heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) system in a new office building. “State of the art” expresses an ambition, but does not by itself give a measurable acceptance criterion.

Connection: NEC4 uses Scope for requirements and constraints. This overview and the specification in card 2 belong to that same Scope; they are not two independent contractual definitions.

What must the system do, and what evidence will show that it does it?

2Detailed Scope · the measurable requirement

Scenario: maintain 68–72°F (about 20–22°C), with separate controls for each floor and a minimum stated efficiency of 18 SEER. These are the original example’s fictional requirements, not a recommended HVAC specification.

Connection: in NEC4 this is Scope; Works Information is the older NEC3 terminology. A real specification would also need an appropriate rating standard, test conditions, operating limits and evidence. “Year-round” alone leaves those conditions unclear.

Could two people interpret the temperature or efficiency requirement differently?

NEC: Works Information becomes Scope.

3Programme · clauses 31 and 32

Scenario: plan installation in weeks 12–15 and testing and commissioning in week 16. The original five-week window includes one week for commissioning.

Connection: the accepted programme supports coordination and assessment of change. Updating it to show a delay does not by itself change the contractual Completion Date; that is a separate contractual process.

Which preceding delivery, access or design decision makes the week-12 start possible?

NEC: the accepted programme and additional time.

4Early warning · clause 15

Scenario: the Contractor learns that the specified unit has a ten-week lead time which could delay installation. Raise the matter promptly so the team can consider earlier ordering, sequencing or another unit.

Connection: NEC4 ECC early warnings sit in clause 15. They bring potential effects on price, time or performance into joint risk management. Giving an early warning does not itself create a compensation event or a payment entitlement.

What action might avoid the delay before anyone assesses its contractual consequences?

NEC4 guidance mapping, clause 15.1; NEC: early warnings and compensation events are distinct (2017 article uses earlier clause numbering).

5Compensation events · clause 60 onwards

Scenario: the Project Manager instructs an upgrade to the Client’s specified HVAC requirements. Assume it is a genuine change to the Client’s Scope, not correction of the Contractor’s non-compliant design.

Connection: a qualifying instruction to change Scope is a compensation event, subject to exceptions. Notification, quotation, assessment and implementation still matter. Its assessed effect may concern time, money, both or neither; it is not simply the difference between two catalogue prices.

What changes in installation, commissioning and planned Completion as well as purchase cost?

NEC: introduction to compensation events; NEC: Scope-change exceptions.

6Price for Work Done to Date · Option A

Scenario: the fictional activity schedule allocates £150,000 of an initial £1,000,000 total to one HVAC installation-and-testing activity. That is 15% by our assumption; NEC does not prescribe a 15% HVAC milestone.

Connection: under Option A, Price for Work Done to Date (PWDD) uses prices for completed activities or groups of activities. Assume this single activity satisfies the contract’s completion test. Part-progress alone does not earn a fraction of that activity’s price.

This activity’s PWDD contribution: £0

This illustration excludes other activities, compensation-event adjustments, retention, tax, prior payments and other amounts due. £150,000 is a contribution to PWDD, not an assessed net payment or permission to invoice.

NEC: Option A pricing and completed activities.

7Defects · definition 11.2(6), clauses 43 and 44

Scenario: testing shows that a zone cannot achieve the temperature range under the agreed test conditions. If the works do not comply with the Scope, that can constitute a Defect; a problem caused by operation outside those conditions is not automatically the same thing.

Connection: NEC4 defines Defects by compliance with Scope and, for Contractor-designed work, relevant law and accepted design. The Contractor and Supervisor have notification duties. The defects date and a notified defect’s correction period are distinct; the Contractor’s obligation to correct is not created only by a notification.

Understand the bargain

ZOPA · possible agreement

The zone of possible agreement is the price interval that leaves each party at least as well off as its alternative. Here the supplier’s minimum is cost + outside-option surplus; the client’s maximum is value − outside-option surplus.

If the minimum exceeds the maximum, no acceptable price exists under these assumptions. Equal limits leave one price and zero additional joint gain. A ZOPA makes agreement possible, not inevitable.

BATNA · the alternative to this deal

The best alternative to a negotiated agreement is what a party can do if this negotiation fails. An alternative supplier, another job, a changed schedule or a reduced scope might matter in a real negotiation.

Here we compress each alternative into a known, non-negative surplus in the same price units. Zero means the no-deal alternative has zero surplus. These are net benefits, not another supplier’s invoice price. Stronger alternatives shrink the ZOPA.

How this exercise settles a price

The ask is a proposed lower bound and the bid a proposed upper bound, not binding offers. The exercise intersects those bounds with the parties’ reservation values.

Lower = max(ask, supplier minimum)
Upper = min(bid, client maximum)

If lower ≤ upper, the model selects their midpoint. Otherwise it records no agreement. The midpoint is an explicit classroom convention, not a prediction, legal rule or proven negotiation equilibrium.

Supplier surplus = price − cost; client surplus = value − price. Each party’s gain over its alternative subtracts that party’s outside-option surplus. In no-deal outcomes each keeps its alternative; incremental gains are zero.

Try the counterexamples
  • Bridge: ask 55 and bid 50 are only five units apart, but those proposed bounds do not overlap.
  • Signalling: matching offers at 50 are below the supplier’s cost of 55.
  • Bridge: ask 40 and bid 60 leave room for a price of 50, giving five units of surplus to each side.
  • Bridge: give each party an outside-option surplus of 6. A 10-unit trading surplus cannot beat 12 units of alternatives.
What the score means

The score counts packages for which this rule found a mutually acceptable price. It does not measure fairness, negotiation skill or project success. A no-deal result can be rational. There is no strategic opponent, uncertainty, procurement competition, package coupling, discounting or repeated bargaining within a package.
