      What the two views reveal

      The network shows who and what each record connects. The geographic projection places those same records using their available coordinates. A contract without delivery coordinates is assigned to an approximate project anchor; an organisation may inherit an office postcode or a city proxy. Several nodes can share a point.

      Grouping records into one project, assigning delivery to an area and inferring a framework from an attachment name are modelling choices. Dashed lines expose those choices. Geographic closeness cannot by itself establish operational collaboration, governance, delivery, or causal influence.

      Scaling this pattern would require notice amendments, identity reconciliation, proper delivery geometry, subcontract tiers and dated snapshots. Those are future extensions, not capabilities of this frozen sample.

      Sources, corrections and modelling choices

      The 54 source nodes and 76 relationships are retained. Contract-to-supplier arrows are now labelled SUPPLIED_BY; delivery-area links are labelled ASSOCIATED_WITH_AREA. Relationship details preserve the original labels. The sample does not merge historical buyer names into a single legal identity.

      Primary notices corrected the gantry award date and contract term, and the hydrogen advisory dates, value and CPV. The finance notice gives different summary and award values; both remain visible. The task-order notice gives different summary and award end dates; both remain visible. Select those cards to inspect the differences.

      Public notice links are attached to the contract cards. For actual project geography and current project information, use the National Highways project pages. Inherited coordinate values were originally attributed to postcode pages on doogal.co.uk and a latitude.to crossing point; they have not been independently geocoded here.
