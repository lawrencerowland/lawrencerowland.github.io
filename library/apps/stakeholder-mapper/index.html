What the map helps you notice

Power means the ability to affect the project; interest means concern or involvement. A high-power person may care little about today’s work, while someone with little formal power may be deeply affected by it.

The four labels are prompts for an engagement conversation. “Manage closely” suggests regular two-way involvement; “Keep satisfied” suggests checking concerns and consequential decisions; “Keep informed” suggests accessible updates and listening; “Monitor” suggests checking for changing interests. Low power is not a reason to exclude someone affected by the project.

Assumptions, limitations and source

You supply the scores. The 0–100 scale and split at 50 are conventions in this example, not measured probabilities or a validated threshold. Moving a score from 49 to 50 changes its category sharply, even though your judgement barely changed. Scores do not measure support, opposition, rights, urgency or relationships between people.

Review the map with the people concerned and as the project changes. The UK Government Analysis Function’s stakeholder-mapping guidance explains the power/interest approach and revisiting assessments. Its contact labels differ slightly; this app retains the original four labels and exposes their simple rule.
