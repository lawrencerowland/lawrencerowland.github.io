Rail Upgrade Monotone Co‑Design Studio

A fresh, documentation-led interactive scenario for a rail transit capacity upgrade: longer platforms, tighter signalling headways, and stronger traction power must be designed together, with staged delivery, contracts, approvals, and political risk treated as part of the co-design challenge rather than an afterthought.

This studio compares supplied programme strategies; the separate staged-path experiment constructs legal action sequences under explicit service constraints.

Method in six moves. The documentation’s discipline is inductive and compositional: define the task, decompose it hierarchically, identify components as design problems, connect them by functionality/resource constraints, model alternatives via catalogues and coproduct-like choices, then query minimal resources. Loops are not banned; they are solved by monotone fixed-point logic.
1 · Define the task
The task here is not “buy some rail assets”. It is: deliver corridor peak throughput under explicit ceilings on budget, time, and programme risk. The page treats this as the top-level function and keeps implementation choices separate from it.
2 · Functional decomposition
We decompose “increase corridor throughput” into longer trains, tighter headways, and stronger traction power, plus a governance sub-task. This respects the documentation’s distinction between what the system must do and how it will eventually do it.
3 · From decomposition to co-design diagram
Each subsystem is modelled as a design problem with green functionality edges and red resource edges. The joins are co-design constraints: e.g. a larger train length forces non-decreasing signalling and power capability.
4 · Populate implementations
The subsystem options are finite catalogues. Different governance modes and sequencing strategies act like alternatives that can be chosen. This gives a discrete, exact, non-differentiable design space without pretending the problem is convex.
5 · Solve by querying minimal resources
For each feasible service plan the solver returns the minimal resource bundles under the product order. In programme language: the result is the set of plans that are not jointly beaten on cost, time, risk, and possessions.
6 · Treat loops explicitly
Mismatch creates approvals risk, risk stretches schedule, and a longer schedule can provoke further risk. The studio resolves that circularity with a monotone iteration starting from the least element, i.e. a directorially legible analogue of Kleene ascent.
Stylised modelling choices in this scenario
Packages, durations, governance effects and penalties are illustrative. Capacity requirement maps are non-decreasing; resource bundles are Pareto-filtered. Sequencing is restricted to four supplied strategies. Readiness is linearly interpolated within work stages and mismatch is sampled at 120 intervals; it is not certified operating service. The feedback calculation is a synthetic scalar approximation, not a general MCDP loop solver.

Glossary

              

                Functionality = what a subsystem provides. 

                Resources = what it requires. 

                Catalogue = arbitrary discrete implementation relation. 

                Choose = alternatives / coproduct. 

                Antichain = mutually non-dominating minimal plans. 

                Loop = recursive co-design constraint solved by least fixed point.
              

            

            

              
Interpretive note

              

                The point of the exercise is not to make rail projects look like a single spreadsheet optimisation. It is to formalise the fact that every local improvement pushes on a wider dependency structure, and that management choices about packaging and pacing are themselves part of the design space.
