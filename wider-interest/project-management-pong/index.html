Keep it in play.

You are the bottom paddle. Hit near its centre, then send the ball to the top paddle without touching a wall: that is a clean transfer.

The challenge is to grow scope from 1.0 to 10.0. Try it in fewer than 52 exchanges, an invented benchmark for this game.

  • Clean transfer +0.4Scope is communicated from project to programme.
  • Programme misses +0.5Work falls back to the project; scope grows.
  • You miss −0.3Requirements are dropped; scope shrinks.
  • Either wall −0.2Problems slow the ball; superficial progress gives it a sideways boost.

A toy, with deliberately awkward incentives. More scope is not more completed work. A programme miss can help you reach the target: that makes it a useful question about the score, not evidence of good management.

The rules, and where the metaphor stops

This is an arcade experiment inspired by project communication. Its numbers are invented; it is not a validated model of project delivery, productivity or programme performance.

  • Every paddle contact counts as one exchange, called a “toy week”. Wall contacts and misses do not add weeks. These are turns in a game, not elapsed time or a project forecast.
  • A clean transfer starts in the middle 60% of your paddle and ends at the programme paddle without a wall contact or miss. Only that completed transfer earns +0.4 scope.
  • The problems wall reduces horizontal speed to 30%; the superficial-progress wall multiplies it by 1.3. Speed limits keep the game playable. Both remove 0.2 scope and break a clean transfer.
  • Scope changes only at these events, never just because time passes. It cannot fall below 0.5. The ball grows with scope and moves more slowly; its area, rather than its radius, follows the score.
  • A miss serves a new ball while keeping the score. Reaching 10.0 ends the game, whether through a clean transfer or work being handed back. There is no time limit and no loss at week 52.
  • The 52-exchange challenge is fictional. It is not an average project duration. The automated programme paddle and optional assistance are simple game opponents, not models of human behaviour.
  • Pause freezes the game. Leaving the page or switching away pauses it automatically. Reset returns every score and the court to the beginning.
