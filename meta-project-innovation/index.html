A team is moving its customer data to the cloud. It has inherited too much scope, incomplete evidence and limited specialist time. Explore five ways to improve the project before optimising its plan.

The enquiry behind the trial

End. Meta Project Innovation seeks ideas that improve project planning and delivery along value, feasibility, surprise and innovation. Its ambition extends beyond optimising a supplied plan.

Concept. The foray already includes demand challenge before planning and claims tied to evidence sensors among its candidate ideas. Those questions provide a conceptual home for this trial’s scope and evidence lenses.

Construction. The fictional cloud migration puts those questions beside whole-package capacity allocation, typed dependency paths and schedule heuristics. Five independent lenses share a current decision ledger.

Limit. The inputs and scores are invented. Software checks cover the stated calculations; they do not demonstrate a better project choice or a delivery benefit. The wider foray ambition remains unfinished.

Try this: choose “Challenge harder”, then compare schedule policies with one and two workstreams. The lenses share a case and a ledger; edits in one lens do not change another lens’s inputs. All figures are invented. Evidence-chain acceptance remains separate from a gate authority’s decision.

Allocate scarce expertise in whole packages

Teams disclose the outcome unlocked, cost of waiting, confidence in that claim, minimum useful package and a credible alternative. The protocol allocates days, not fictional money.

A greedy heuristic ranks a score per day, then funds each whole package that fits. It can leave a better combination unfunded; it does not find a general optimum. The weights are invented scenario choices.

See the allocation rule

Score per day = (delay cost × confidence / 100 × alternative factor + reuse × 0.55) / minimum days. The alternative factor is 1.15 for none, 1 for partial and 0.82 for yes. Fixed reuse points: S1 = 4, S2 = 2, S3 = 3, S4 = 5. Ties retain S1–S4 order. A skipped package does not consume capacity. Zero confidence removes the delay term but leaves the assumed reuse term.

For example, with 4 days, a 3-day package scoring 3 per day is picked before two 2-day packages scoring 2.5 each: its total score is 9, while the pair would score 10. A ranking is a discussion aid, not a proof of the best allocation.

Run a transparent planner tournament

Five declared heuristics schedule eight dependency-constrained tasks under a base case and two duration stresses. Each task uses one workstream until it finishes. The app compares the resulting schedules.

Lower regret is better under this invented score. Regret compares only these five heuristics in each scenario, then averages the three differences equally. “Balanced” is a weighted priority rule; no robust optimisation or calibrated forecast is implemented.

Base-scenario schedule

See the policy rules and scenario changes

At each finish time, all completed tasks release capacity; eligible tasks are ranked using their scenario duration. Ties use task ID order. Tasks are indivisible, never pre-empted and start as soon as capacity and all predecessors allow. A task marked “outcome” is assumed to yield its outcome when finished; the app does not test actual validation.

  • Risk-first: 2 × risk + uncertainty − 0.2 × duration.
  • Value-first: 2 × value + 0.4 × risk − 0.2 × duration.
  • Learning-first: 2 × uncertainty + 0.5 × risk − 0.2 × duration.
  • Reuse-first: 2 × reuse + 0.4 × value − 0.2 × duration.
  • Balanced heuristic (formerly “Robust balance”): risk + value + uncertainty + reuse − 0.45 × duration.

Scenario score = 2 × finish week + 3 × first outcome week + 1.2 × unresolved uncertainty at week 6 + 0.18 × Σ(risk × task finish week) − 0.65 × reuse completed by week 6. All weights and task points are synthetic. Uncertainty is the sum of points on all unfinished tasks, not a critical-path measure; reuse is credited only on completion.

Data stress adds 2 weeks to P1 and 1 week to P3. Access-control stress adds 2 weeks to P2 and 1 week to P7. These changes are known to the scheduling rule before it starts; this is not adaptive replanning after a surprise.
